Speculative · unconstrained · sequenced at the end

If the audit fixed what DzPharm is, this is what it could become.

Roughly seventy ideas across design, intelligence, clinical depth, community and distribution — grounded in what the platform already does well, and in what nobody else building for the Algerian market has tried. Every idea is tagged Now, Next or Long bet so nothing here is a mystery about when to act on it.

A caveat worth stating plainly: not everything below should be built, and several ideas below actively compete with each other for the same slice of a pharmacist's attention. Treat this as a menu assembled by someone who knows the kitchen, not a mandate. Part I still tells you what to do this month.

BIG SWING 01

The compatibility layer

A halal/haram, alcohol-content and Ramadan-fasting compatibility flag on every one of the 9,555 records — the one clinical dimension no European, American or Gulf platform will ever build correctly for this market, because it is not a translation problem, it is a jurisprudence problem.

BIG SWING 02

A Copilot that speaks first

Today the AI only answers. It could notice — an expiring EpiPen in a Family Cabinet, a watched shortage coming back into stock, a Ramadan schedule that needs re-timing next week — and say so before it is asked.

BIG SWING 03

The counter, not the tab

Barcode scan → Chifa card read → interaction check → printed handout, as one motion at the point of dispensing. DzPharm stops being the reference a pharmacist checks and becomes the software the counter runs on.

Section 01

Visual identity, taken further

The existing system — 400 tokens, 20 palettes, 118 icons, AAA contrast — is already the platform's second moat. These ideas extend it rather than replace it: new palette directions grounded in specific places and materials rather than generic hues, a first pass at the pharmacy-specific icons Lucide simply does not have, and the case for sound and print as design surfaces nobody has touched yet.

Four new Nuancier directions

The current twenty palettes already carry named cultural identities. These four extend the idea toward specific materials and places rather than moods — each is one degree more literal than "Sky Blue" or "Sahara Warm," which makes them easier to explain to a pharmacist choosing between them.

Zellige NuitCobalt tilework against ochre grout, the way Algiers courtyards read after dark. A cooler, more architectural alternative to the default Sky palette for users who find blue too soft for a clinical tool.
HammamWarm plaster, terracotta, sage soap. Deliberately softer and less clinical than every other palette — proposed as the default for Mode famille, where a patient should feel spoken to, not diagnosed.
Tell VertThe Kabyle highlands rather than the Sahara — every existing palette leans desert or coastal-blue. A forest green gives the botanical mode a home that is not borrowed from the clinical palette next door.
Souk CuivreHammered copper and brass against dark leather. A high-warmth option for the professional mode that reads as premium rather than sterile — copper is the Maghreb's own metal in a way that gold or silver are not.
One rule that must survive every future palette

Whatever gets added, the seven clinical severity colours stay locked outside the Nuancier's reach — that is P0-12 in the audit, and it applies retroactively to anything proposed here.

The icons Lucide doesn't have

General-purpose icon sets have no symbol for a Chifa card, an AMM seal, a suppository or a wilaya. These nine are direction sketches only — a designer should redraw them properly — but they show the grid, stroke weight and level of abstraction the existing 118 icons already establish, so anything added feels native rather than bolted on.

Motion as a safety channel, not decoration

  • One signature loader, everywhere. Replace every generic spinner with a single two-tone capsule motif that splits and rejoins — one motion, recognisable at a glance, used consistently instead of invented per screen.
  • Protect the beacon-ping. The pulsing emergency-banner dot already exists and works. The rule going forward: that pulse is reserved for genuine urgency and is never reused for a promotional badge or a notification dot — the moment it means two things, it stops meaning either.
  • One orchestrated moment, not scattered ones. A single well-built animation when a shortage a user is watching comes back into stock — a quiet checkmark resolve, nothing more — does more than ten small hover flourishes across the interface, and respects P1-14's reduced-motion rule by design.

Sound, used once, deliberately

A pharmacy counter is visually busy — a screen half-glanced-at while hands are elsewhere. This is one of the few clinical contexts where a sound cue genuinely earns its place, provided it stays optional and rare.

  • A soft two-tone chime on a successful barcode scan, distinct from any error tone.
  • A single low, unmistakable tone — the only sound the platform makes without being asked — reserved solely for a contre-indication absolue result in the interaction checker. Off by default, one toggle, and never used for anything else.

Print as its own designed surface

  • A patient handout re-cut to the width of a folded pharmacy bag receipt, not a shrunk A4 page — this is genuinely how it will be carried home.
  • A pictogram-only variant — sun and moon for morning and evening, a plate for with food, a crossed plate for without — for the significant share of patients for whom French or Arabic text is not the fastest read.
Section 02

The intelligence layer, extended

The Copilot is already the platform's most differentiated feature — trilingual, Algeria-grounded, structured. These ideas move it from answering questions to anticipating them, and from asserting answers to showing its work.

Big swing 02, in full

A Copilot that notices before it is asked

Every one of the platform's data sources already knows things worth surfacing: the Family Cabinet knows an EpiPen expires in nine days, the shortage centre knows a watched drug just came back into stock, the Ramadan adapter knows a patient's schedule needs re-timing before next Friday, the interaction checker knows a saved list contains a drug that was just placed on a national recall. None of this reaches the user unless they go looking. A proactive layer — a quiet "Pour vous" panel on the dashboard, populated from existing data with no new clinical judgement required — turns four already-built features into one continuously useful assistant instead of four tools a pharmacist has to remember to check.

Next

Show its work

Every Copilot answer carries a citation chip back to the specific RCP or monograph passage it drew from. Turns "trust me" into "verify me" — the single highest-leverage trust feature available, because the underlying content already exists.

Next

Say when it isn't sure

Out-of-dataset or genuinely ambiguous dosing questions get an explicit "hors du référentiel — vérifiez auprès d'un spécialiste" rather than a confidently formatted wrong answer. Confidence should be a signal the model gives, not a tone it defaults to.

Next

Deuxième avis

A button beside any answer that reruns the query with a different framing and flags disagreement between the two runs. Cheap to build once streaming exists, and valuable exactly on the highest-stakes questions.

Long bet

Multi-turn case solver

A persistent patient-case object — age, comorbidities, current regimen — that the Copilot reasons against across an entire shift, not just one exchange. "Now add clarithromycin" should not require restating the rest of the case.

Long bet

Prescription OCR

Photograph a paper prescription; extract drug names, doses and frequencies; cross-check automatically against interactions and the posology database. The paste-a-prescription tool already discovered mid-audit is the natural stepping stone — ship that properly first.

Long bet

Pill and packaging recognition

Photograph an unidentified tablet or box; match colour, shape, imprint and packaging against the 9,555-record image set. The single most valuable feature for a poison-centre call about an unlabelled medication.

Next

Formulary-aware substitution

A pharmacy uploads its stock list; when the interaction checker or the generics simulator suggests an alternative, it is silently ranked by what that specific pharmacy already has on the shelf.

Now

Instant-answer cache

Pre-compute and edge-cache the top few hundred recurring questions — common paediatric doses, Liste I vs Liste II, standard interaction pairs — so the most-asked questions return in under 200ms with no model call at all, and keep answering during a Gemini outage.

Now

Register-matching, fully

A Darija question gets a Darija answer, not Modern Standard Arabic — the one-line system-prompt fix from the audit, extended: French formal vs familiar, and patient-mode simplicity, should all mirror how the question was actually asked.

Long bet

WhatsApp Copilot

The same grounded assistant, reachable where Algerian pharmacists already live professionally. A quick "posologie amoxicilline enfant 16kg" answered inside a chat they never leave.

Section 03

New clinical & professional tools

The existing thirteen-tool suite is unusually deep for a market of this size. These extend the same rigour into gaps the current menu leaves open — including one idea, the compatibility layer, that no competitor anywhere can replicate without genuine local expertise.

Big swing 01, in full

The compatibility layer

Add three quiet fields to every one of the 9,555 records: whether the capsule shell is gelatin-based and of what origin, whether the formulation contains alcohol, and whether the dosing regimen can be adapted to fast safely during Ramadan. Surface all three as a single badge on the medication card, reviewed by a qualified religious-affairs advisor working alongside the clinical team — this is a jurisprudence question as much as a pharmacology one, and it deserves the same rigour as a dosing calculation. Every existing Ramadan chronopharmacology feature already proves the team understands this territory; this generalises it from one tool into a property of the entire dataset. No European, American or Gulf platform will build this correctly for the Algerian market, because it is not a translation exercise — it requires exactly the local expertise this team already has.

Next

Hepatic dose adjustment

Child-Pugh score in, dose adjustment out — for hepatotoxic drugs, the mirror image of the existing renal calculator and built on the same pattern.

Next

Anticoagulation dosing helper

INR-based Warfarin dose adjustment with a titration schedule — a genuinely dangerous class to get wrong and currently absent from the toolset.

Long bet

Antibiogram by wilaya

Crowdsourced local resistance patterns — "E. coli resistance to amoxicillin in Alger: 47%" — invaluable for empiric therapy and impossible for any dataset-only competitor to replicate.

Long bet

IV compatibility matrix

Y-site compatibility for hospital-pharmacy IV combinations. Not needed by every user, essential to the ones who need it, and currently offered by nobody in the market.

Now

Growth-chart plotter

WHO paediatric growth curves wired directly into the existing posology calculator, so a weight percentile and a dose calculation happen in the same screen instead of two separate tools.

Next

Vaccine schedule tracker

The Algerian PEV calendar, tied to Family Cabinet member profiles, with due-date reminders — turns a static reference into something a parent returns to every few months.

Long bet

Pharmacovigilance channel

Adverse-reaction reporting routed directly to the Algerian pharmacovigilance centre. Turns DzPharm from a reference tool into actual regulatory infrastructure — a materially larger claim, and one worth making carefully.

Next

Adherence tracking

Simple trend lines for chronic conditions — diabetes, hypertension — inside the Family Cabinet, built from data the household is already entering.

Long bet

Travel health advisor

Required vaccinations and malaria prophylaxis by destination, relevant for Hajj, Umrah and labour migration — a genuinely underserved need in this market.

Next

Recall & withdrawal alerts

The dataset already flags withdrawn products; turn that flag into a push notification for anyone who has favourited or recently viewed the affected drug, rather than a fact only visible on demand.

Now

Storage & cold-chain badges

A small icon set — refrigerate, protect from light, keep below 25°C — surfaced on every record, feeding directly from the Family Cabinet's existing seasonal-heat advice into something visible everywhere, not just there.

Section 04

Search, reimagined

The audit already fixes fuzzy matching and history. These push discovery further — toward how a pharmacist actually thinks under time pressure, rather than how a keyword index thinks.

Next

Ask the search bar a question

"Que puis-je donner à une femme enceinte contre les maux de tête ?" typed directly into the header search, not just inside the Copilot — the same grounded reasoning, reached from where people are already looking.

Long bet

Photo search

The pill-recognition idea from Section 02, surfaced as a camera icon inside the search bar itself rather than buried in a separate flow.

Next

Hover-to-simplify jargon

Hovering or tapping a clinical term inside an RCP — "myélosuppression," "clairance" — surfaces a plain-language gloss inline, without leaving the page.

Now

Cross-mapped synonyms

A DCI-to-local-name-to-common-misspelling table so "amoxicilline," "amoxiciline" and أموكسيسيلين all resolve to the same record — cheaper than full fuzzy matching and catches the errors people actually make.

Now

Zero-result logging as a data-gap map

Every search that returns nothing is logged and reviewed — it is simultaneously a bug list and a live map of exactly where the dataset or the synonym table needs expanding.

Next

Voice from the header

The dictation work already scoped for the Copilot, exposed as a header-level entry point so voice becomes how someone starts a session, not only how they continue one.

Section 05

Community, supply & network effects

Three features already brainstormed in the source material — shortage reports, a heatmap, an inventory marketplace — belong to one coherent story: DzPharm as the layer that lets pharmacies see each other, not just the dataset.

Next

National shortage heatmap

The nine live community reports, mapped by wilaya and filterable by drug class, so a pharmacist sees which medications are tight in their region this week — not just that a report exists somewhere.

Long bet

Inter-pharmacy surplus marketplace

Verified pharmacies list surplus stock for transfer to a neighbour facing a stockout. Reduces waste on one side and closes a gap on the other, and it only works once accounts and verification exist.

Long bet

Verified-pharmacist Q&A

Edge cases the Copilot cannot confidently answer get posted to a moderated peer network — and every good answer becomes training material for the next version of the Copilot itself.

Next

Anonymous peer benchmarking

"Your top-10 dispensed medications versus your wilaya's average" — professional insight without turning clinical work into a leaderboard. Kept deliberately quiet and private, never public or competitive.

Long bet

CME credit tracking

Time spent in clinical tools and Copilot consultations logged as continuing-education credit, subject to medical council approval — turns daily use into a professional-development record.

Section 06

Data products & business models

The dataset and the AI are the moat; these are ways to extend the reach of both without diluting either. All three explicitly wait for accounts and governance — data ethics come before data revenue.

Long bet

Public read API

Free for academic researchers, paid for commercial integrations. Converts the dataset into both a revenue line and a defensive moat — every integration built on it makes the platform harder to displace.

Long bet

Open data exports

A monthly CSV or JSON export of the AMM registry under a permissive licence, credited back to DzPharm — builds goodwill and an ecosystem at low cost.

Long bet

National statistics dashboard

Anonymised search and consultation trends by wilaya and season — genuinely valuable public-health signal, and the item most dependent on the account system and a clear governance policy existing first.

Next

Embeddable search widget

A "search powered by DzPharm" box that other Algerian health sites or hospital intranets can drop in — extends reach without extending headcount.

Long bet

B2B inventory layer

The same dataset repurposed as an actual stock-management add-on for pharmacies — monetises the operational side of the business, not only the reference side.

Section 07

Inclusive by design

The audit's accessibility fixes make the current French UI compliant. These go further — toward audiences the interface does not reach at all today.

Next

Full Arabic UI

Not just readable Arabic content — a complete right-to-left interface built on CSS logical properties from the start, so it is a real experience rather than a mirrored afterthought.

Long bet

Tamazight support

Genuinely novel — no pharmaceutical platform anywhere has done this. A meaningful signal for Kabylie and the broader Amazigh-speaking population, and a first worth being first at.

Next

Low-literacy patient mode

Icon-first dosing instructions — sun and moon for morning and evening, a plate for with food — for the real share of patients who read pictures faster than paragraphs, in any language.

Next

Voice-only flow

Beyond screen-reader compliance: a genuinely voice-driven path through search and the Copilot for visually impaired users, not just a keyboard-navigable version of the visual one.

Section 08

Beyond the browser tab

Where DzPharm shows up matters as much as what it contains — and in this market, a meaningful share of the audience is not reachable by a native app at all.

Big swing 03, in full

The counter, not the tab

Every piece already exists in isolation: a working barcode scanner, a Chifa simulator, an interactions checker, a print system. Chained into a single guided flow at the point of dispensing — scan the box, read the card, check interactions automatically against whatever else is already on the counter, print the handout — DzPharm stops being a reference a pharmacist opens in a spare moment and becomes the software the counter actually runs on. That is a materially different product, and it is built entirely from features that already work.

Long bet

WhatsApp Business bot

Quick drug queries answered inside the channel Algerian pharmacists already use all day, every day — meeting the workflow rather than asking it to change.

Long bet

SMS / USSD fallback

A basic query-and-answer path for feature phones. Not every pharmacist or patient carries a smartphone, and this is the one channel that reaches all of them.

Next

True offline mode

The full 9,555-record dataset and the core calculators usable with zero connectivity — valuable everywhere, essential for rural pharmacies on unreliable connections, and a prerequisite the audit already flags before any native app is wrapped.

Long bet

Native iOS / Android

Worth doing once routing, accounts and offline mode are solid — in that order, and not before, so the app store review meets a finished product rather than a work in progress.

Section 09

The small moments

None of these move a grade on their own. Together they are the difference between a tool that works and a tool people are quietly fond of.

Now

Teaching empty states, everywhere

The Interactions module's empty state — explain the value, then offer the action — is already excellent. Extend that exact pattern to every empty screen on the platform instead of inventing a new one each time.

Next

A quiet, automatic Ramadan mode

A subtle, reversible visual shift each Ramadan, and the chronopharmacology tool nudged forward in the navigation for the month — present without becoming a decoration that outstays its welcome.

Next

A September nudge

The vaccine tracker surfaces itself briefly at back-to-school time, when it is genuinely most relevant — one well-timed prompt instead of a permanent banner.

Now

A counter-night mode

A compact, low-glare density setting for a dim pharmacy after dark — the density system already exists in the store and simply needs a switch, which the audit already scopes.

The one thing to protect

Everything in this document assumes the audit's foundation lands first — routing, accounts, a trustworthy Copilot. Building the compatibility layer or the proactive assistant on top of a platform that still 404s on direct URLs would be building a second storey on a foundation that hasn't cured. Sequence discipline is the actual innovation here; the ideas are the easy part.
